First-Principles · Quantum Materials · Multiferroics

Ying Zhou

I am currently a Specially Appointed Associate Professor in the School of Sciences at Zhejiang Sci-Tech University. I obtained my Ph.D. in Physics from the School of Physics at Southeast University (SEU), supervised by Prof. Shuai Dong, Dean of the School of Physics and Recipient of the National Science Fund for Distinguished Young Scholars.

My research focuses on the theoretical design and first-principles calculations of novel quantum materials, exploring their microscopic mechanisms, magnetoelectric coupling effects, and quantum state manipulation.

01 First-Principles Calculations

Microscopic mechanisms of magnetic, ferroelectric, and multiferroic materials.

02 Magnetoelectric Coupling

Spin-dependent transport and quantum state manipulation via external fields.

03 2D Multiferroics

Out-of-plane ferroelectricity, altermagnetism, and piezomagnetic effects in 2D layered perovskites.

04 Topological Magnetoelectric Effects

Topologically protected inverse magnetoelectric effects and room-temperature ferromagnetism.

🔬 Research Experience

2024–2025

Out-of-plane ferroelectricity, altermagnetism, and piezomagnetism in 2D layered perovskites

Stabilized out-of-plane ferroelectricity in two-dimensional Ruddlesden–Popper phases, identified switchable persistent spin textures, and revealed strain-driven Jahn–Teller piezomagnetism and magnetoelectric coupling.

2022–2024

Topological inverse magnetoelectric effects and room-temperature multiferroicity

Identified a topologically protected inverse magnetoelectric effect in two-dimensional GdI2 and predicted room-temperature ferromagnetism and record magnetically driven polarization in 5d halide OsX2 monolayers.

2021–2022

Spin-induced ferroelectricity and ferromagnetism in 2D double perovskites

Demonstrated coexistence and coupling of spin-induced out-of-plane ferroelectricity and ferromagnetism above room temperature, and explained their microscopic origin through spin-dependent p–d hybridization.

2019–2021

Octahedral-rotation-induced hybrid improper ferroelectricity in 2D perovskites

Established how octahedral rotations and tilting govern lattice dynamics, polarization reversal, and strain- or interface-controlled magnetoelectric pathways in two-dimensional perovskite oxides.

Collaborative Research

2024–2025

Strain-induced room-temperature ferrimagnetism and polar phases in 3d–4d double perovskites

Collaborative first-principles work revealed how compressive strain strengthens Co–O–Ru superexchange and how octahedral-rotation gradients break glide symmetry to stabilize a switchable polar phase.

2024–2025

Interfacial charge transfer and magnetic control in nitride/oxide heterostructures

Clarified charge transfer from Ni3InN to SrVO3, the resulting layer-resolved magnetic moments, and the possibility of inducing non-collinear magnetism at the heterointerface.
